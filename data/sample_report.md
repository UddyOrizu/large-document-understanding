# Meridian Health Group — FY2025 Annual Report & Corporate Policy Manual

## 1. Executive Summary

Meridian Health Group is a diversified healthcare services provider operating across
outpatient diagnostics, occupational health, and specialty pharmacy distribution. This
report combines our FY2025 annual results with the corporate policy manual referenced
throughout the business, and is intended for internal circulation to management,
finance, HR, and IT security teams.

Meridian Health Group's total revenue for FY2025 was £412.6 million, up 8.3% year-on-year,
driven primarily by growth in the diagnostics segment and the continued rollout of our
occupational health contracts across the Midlands and North West regions. Operating
margin improved to 14.1%, from 12.7% in FY2024, reflecting cost discipline in
procurement and a full year of synergies from the Ashcombe Diagnostics acquisition
completed in March 2024.

The board approved a final dividend of 9.2 pence per share, bringing the total FY2025
dividend to 14.5 pence per share, a 6% increase on the prior year. Headcount grew from
3,140 to 3,410 full-time equivalents over the course of the year, with the majority of
new hires concentrated in diagnostics and in the expanding international assignments
programme supporting our new Dublin and Rotterdam clinical partnerships.

This document sets out not only our financial performance but also the operational
policies that govern how the business runs day to day: human resources policy,
information security policy, and vendor and procurement policy. These sections are
binding on all Meridian Health Group employees and contractors and supersede any
previous version of this manual.

## 2. Company Overview

### 2.1 History and Structure

Meridian Health Group was founded in 2008 as a single diagnostics clinic in Manchester
and has grown, through a combination of organic expansion and seven acquisitions, into
a group with 46 sites across the United Kingdom and Ireland, plus a new clinical
partnership footprint in continental Europe established in FY2025. The group is
structured into three operating divisions: Diagnostics (58% of FY2025 revenue),
Occupational Health (31% of FY2025 revenue), and Specialty Pharmacy Distribution (11%
of FY2025 revenue).

The group's registered office is in Manchester, with regional hubs in Leeds, Bristol,
and Glasgow. The executive team comprises the Chief Executive Officer, Chief Financial
Officer, Chief Medical Officer, Chief Information Officer, and Chief People Officer,
each supported by divisional managing directors.

### 2.2 Markets Served

Meridian Health Group serves three principal customer groups: NHS trusts and integrated
care boards, who account for approximately 47% of group revenue; corporate occupational
health clients, who account for approximately 34% of group revenue; and private
pharmacy and specialty distribution customers, who account for the remaining 19%.

Our NHS relationships are typically governed by three- to five-year framework
agreements, while corporate occupational health contracts are usually annual with
automatic renewal clauses. The group has no single customer that accounts for more
than 6% of total revenue, which the board considers an important element of revenue
resilience.

### 2.3 Governance and Strategic Priorities

Meridian Health Group's governance model is designed to balance operational agility with financial discipline across a multi-site, multi-divisional healthcare platform. The board retains responsibility for strategic direction, capital allocation, risk appetite, and approval of major investments, while the executive committee oversees the delivery of annual operating plans, regulatory obligations, and cross-group transformation projects. The board receives quarterly updates on clinical quality, commercial performance, cyber risk, workforce capacity, and procurement exposure, ensuring that management information is aligned to the risk profile of the group.

In FY2025, strategic priorities included expanding the diagnostics network, strengthening occupational health contracting in the Midlands and North West, deepening international partnerships in Dublin and Rotterdam, and investing in secure clinical data infrastructure. These initiatives were intended to improve service resilience, support growth in high-margin healthcare services, and reduce dependence on any single client or region. Management also emphasised the importance of clinical governance, disciplined supplier management, and consistent operational standards across the group, recognising that patient trust and regulatory compliance are critical to the sustainability of the business.

Several strategic programmes were progressed during the year. The roll-out of a standardised clinical operating model across diagnostics sites improved throughput and scheduling efficiency. In occupational health, the group expanded its service cadence for employers seeking preventive health, statutory occupational screening, and on-site health surveillance. In specialty pharmacy distribution, the group continued to refine ordering and logistics processes to manage a more complex distribution footprint while reducing stock wastage and service interruptions. These initiatives were supported by investment in digital reporting platforms, predictive maintenance of imaging equipment, and improved workforce planning tools.

### 2.4 Operational Footprint and Service Model

The group's operating model combines local service delivery with central oversight. Each division retains responsibility for day-to-day client servicing, clinical quality, and workforce management within its own operating framework, while corporate functions such as finance, legal, procurement, technology, and HR provide common standards, governance, and specialist expertise. This approach allows the business to operate at scale without losing site-level accountability, while supporting consistent treatment pathways, contract compliance, and cost management.

Meridian Health Group's diagnostics operations include imaging, pathology-related services, and associated clinical support functions. The occupational health division delivers workplace health assessments, pre-employment screening, disability and absence management support, and health surveillance interventions across commercial clients and public sector organisations. The specialty pharmacy division distributes medicines, specialist treatments, and associated ancillary supplies, with service levels calibrated to patient demand, prescribing patterns, and regulatory requirements.

The business has also developed a network of regional delivery hubs to support service continuity. Manchester remains the primary corporate and governance centre, while Leeds, Bristol, and Glasgow function as operational and logistics hubs for regional activity. This geographic spread supports continuity of service in the event of local disruption, while reducing reliance on a single site for key functions. The new continental European clinical partnership network is expected to widen the group's operational reach, support knowledge transfer, and allow access to new patient pathways in markets where health systems are increasingly shaped by cross-border care solutions.

## 3. Financial Performance

### 3.1 Revenue by Segment

Diagnostics segment revenue was £239.3 million in FY2025, up 11.2% year-on-year, with
volume growth of 7.8% and price/mix effects contributing the remainder. Occupational
Health segment revenue was £127.9 million, up 4.1% year-on-year, reflecting steady
contract renewals but softer new-business volumes in the second half of the year.
Specialty Pharmacy Distribution revenue was £45.4 million, broadly flat year-on-year
at 0.9% growth, as the division absorbed one-off costs associated with a warehouse
relocation in Warrington.

### 3.2 Cost Structure

Cost of sales for FY2025 was £267.8 million, representing 64.9% of revenue, an
improvement of 130 basis points from FY2024. Staff costs, the largest single cost
category, were £198.4 million, up 9.7% year-on-year, broadly tracking headcount
growth plus a 3.5% average pay award effective from April 2025. Property and
facilities costs were £31.2 million. IT and software licensing costs were £14.7
million, up 22% year-on-year, reflecting continued investment in the group's clinical
data platform and information security programme, both described further in Section 6.

### 3.3 Capital Expenditure

Total capital expenditure for FY2025 was £28.6 million, of which £16.1 million was
invested in diagnostic imaging equipment, £6.9 million in the Warrington warehouse
relocation, and £5.6 million in IT infrastructure, including the access control and
logging systems referenced in Section 6.1. The board has approved a further £34 million
of capital expenditure for FY2026, subject to quarterly review against milestones.

### 3.4 Segment Margin and Productivity

Segment-level performance reflects both operational mix and efficiency improvements during FY2025. Diagnostics generated the largest contribution to group profit, supported by increased throughput, stronger utilisation of imaging capacity, and procurement discipline across consumables and service contracts. Occupational Health delivered stable contribution growth supported by contract retention, although expansion in this division was constrained in the second half by slower than expected new-business wins and higher recruitment activity in specialist functions. Specialty Pharmacy Distribution remained relatively flat from a revenue perspective but continued to improve operational discipline in warehousing, distribution cadence, and inventory control.

Operating margin expanded by 140 basis points as the group benefited from a full-year contribution from acquired diagnostics operations, lower input costs in selected categories, and improved manufacturing and logistics forecasting. The company also benefited from central procurement savings and a reduction in duplicated administrative overheads following the integration of the Ashcombe Diagnostics business. Management noted that incremental margin improvement is expected to be more selective in FY2026, given planned investment in clinical workforce capacity, cyber resilience, and digital infrastructure.

### 3.5 Cash Flow and Working Capital

Meridian Health Group reported a strong operating cash conversion profile in FY2025, aided by disciplined debtor management, a stable payment cycle with major NHS and corporate clients, and improved forecasting across the operating divisions. Net cash generated from operations was sufficient to support the group's capital expenditure programme, dividend payments, and incremental investments in clinical infrastructure without materially increasing leverage.

The group maintains a working capital framework designed to align stock levels to contract demand and service-level obligations, while managing exposure to seasonal variations in diagnostics and occupational health demand. Inventory management remains a key focus in the specialty pharmacy business, particularly given the warehouse relocation in Warrington and the need to maintain continuity of supply for regulated medicines and ancillary supplies. The finance function uses monthly reviews of aged receivables, supplier payments, and contract milestone profiles to manage liquidity and project risk.

### 3.6 Capital Allocation and Shareholder Return

The board's approach to capital allocation prioritises reinvestment into clinical capacity, digital systems, and compliance infrastructure, while maintaining a sustainable dividend and protecting liquidity. The FY2025 investment programme included diagnostic equipment replacement, warehouse infrastructure, and IT systems required to support secure access, resilience, and data integration. The approved FY2026 capital programme of £34 million reflects ongoing investment priorities rather than a step-change in expansion strategy; the board has signalled that capital will be released in staged tranches tied to operational milestones and governance review.

The dividend policy continues to balance long-term strategic investment with shareholder returns. The FY2025 dividend rise to 14.5 pence per share reflected a strong underlying earnings base and the board's confidence in the group's operational resilience. Management has indicated that while future changes in dividend levels will remain subject to cash generation, the board expects to maintain a progressive approach provided the business continues to deliver disciplined growth and maintains adequate covenant headroom.

## 4. Risk Factors

### 4.1 Regulatory Risk

Meridian Health Group operates in a heavily regulated environment. Our diagnostics and
occupational health divisions are subject to Care Quality Commission registration
requirements in England, and equivalent regulatory frameworks in Scotland, Wales, and
the Republic of Ireland. Any material adverse finding by a regulator could affect our
ability to bid for NHS framework contracts, which represent a significant proportion of
group revenue as set out in Section 2.2.

### 4.2 Operational Risk

Key operational risks include clinical staffing shortages, particularly among
radiographers and specialist nurses; supply chain disruption affecting diagnostic
consumables; and information security incidents affecting patient data. The group
maintains a dedicated clinical risk committee, chaired by the Chief Medical Officer,
which meets monthly and reports quarterly to the board.

### 4.3 Market Risk

The group's revenue is moderately sensitive to NHS commissioning cycles and corporate
client budget decisions. A significant portion of costs, particularly staff costs, are
denominated in sterling, while a growing proportion of the international assignments
programme, described in Section 5.3, introduces limited euro-denominated cost exposure
that the group does not currently hedge, given its relatively small scale.

### 4.4 Workforce and Clinical Quality Risk

Workforce constraints are among the most material risks facing the group. Demand for radiographers, specialist nurses, and occupational health professionals remains elevated, while competition for skilled clinicians remains intense across the UK and Ireland. In some regions, recruitment delays can extend lead times for new site openings, service expansion, or maintenance cycles for equipment-intensive services. To mitigate this risk, the group has implemented targeted recruitment campaigns, training support initiatives, and retention measures linked to internal progression and specialist clinical development.

Clinical quality is a core board consideration. The company maintains incident reporting, patient safety reviews, and periodic service audits across its clinical operations. In diagnostics, quality assurance includes calibration checks, imaging chain validation, and compliance with clinical protocols. In occupational health programmes, quality controls extend to screening accuracy, careful case review, and the secure management of employee health records. The group also tracks adverse events and near misses to improve risk mitigation across service lines.

### 4.5 Cybersecurity and Third-Party Risk

The risk of cyber disruption is significant in a healthcare environment increasingly dependent on electronic records, image repositories, and cloud-connected systems. Meridian Health Group recognises that operational continuity can be affected not only by external attack but also by supplier compromise, configuration drift, and weak identity hygiene. The company therefore maintains layered controls across endpoint protection, privileged access management, patch discipline, and network segmentation.

Third-party exposure also remains important. The group relies on suppliers for software platforms, clinical consumables, imaging equipment services, cloud infrastructure, and payroll or benefits administration. This creates dependency on service reliability, data protection practices, and contractual resilience. Supplier assessment includes security due diligence, resilience testing, and review of subcontracting arrangements where appropriate.

## 5. Human Resources Policy

This section sets out Meridian Health Group's HR policies as they apply to all
employees, effective from 1 April 2025. It supersedes all previous HR policy documents.

### 5.1 Annual and Sick Leave

Full-time employees are entitled to 25 days of annual leave per year, rising to 28
days after five continuous years of service, in addition to UK bank holidays.
Part-time employees accrue leave on a pro-rata basis. Sick leave is paid at full pay
for the first 20 working days of absence in any rolling 12-month period, and at half
pay for a further 20 working days, subject to submission of appropriate medical
certification after the seventh consecutive day of absence.

### 5.2 Domestic Relocation Allowance

Employees who relocate more than 50 miles within the United Kingdom or Ireland to take
up a new role at Meridian Health Group are eligible for a domestic relocation
allowance of up to £1,200, covering removal costs, temporary accommodation of up to
two weeks, and reasonable travel expenses. Claims must be submitted with receipts
within three months of the relocation date and are approved by the relevant divisional
managing director.

### 5.3 International Assignments

Meridian Health Group's international assignments programme supports employees taking
up temporary or permanent postings at our clinical partnership sites in Dublin and
Rotterdam, as well as any future international sites approved by the board. This
subsection sets out the allowances and conditions that apply.

Employees on assignments of less than six months are classified as short-term
assignees and are not eligible for the settling-in allowance described below; instead,
they receive a per diem of £85 for each day spent at the overseas site, intended to
cover meals and incidental expenses, paid monthly in arrears through standard payroll.

Employees relocating internationally for assignments longer than six months receive a
one-time settling-in allowance of £4,750, paid within the first payroll cycle after
arrival at the overseas site. This allowance is intended to cover initial costs such as
opening a local bank account, securing temporary housing, and other practical
relocation expenses, and is separate from and in addition to any removal costs
reimbursement.

In addition to the settling-in allowance, long-term international assignees receive a
monthly housing supplement of £1,650 for the duration of the assignment, home leave
flights to the UK or Ireland twice per year for the assignee and up to two dependants,
and a monthly cost-of-living adjustment that is reviewed quarterly by the finance
function based on published exchange rate and inflation data for the host country.
Tuition reimbursement of up to £8,000 per academic year is available for dependent
children attending international schools at the host location, subject to prior
approval from the Chief People Officer.

International assignees remain enrolled in the UK pension scheme for the duration of
their assignment unless local regulations require otherwise, and continue to accrue
annual leave under the terms set out in Section 5.1. Assignments are reviewed annually
and may be extended, converted to a permanent local contract, or concluded with a
return to a UK or Ireland-based role, subject to business need and mutual agreement.

### 5.4 Benefits and Wellbeing

All employees are automatically enrolled in the group private medical insurance
scheme after a three-month qualifying period, with the option to add dependants at
their own cost. The group also provides an employee assistance programme offering
confidential counselling and financial guidance, a cycle-to-work scheme, and an annual
wellbeing allowance of £150 that can be claimed against gym membership, fitness
classes, or approved wellbeing apps.

### 5.5 Flexible Working and Attendance

Meridian Health Group supports flexible working arrangements where operationally feasible, particularly for corporate, administrative, and specialist roles. Flexible working can include compressed hours, hybrid arrangements, job sharing, or adjusted start and finish times, subject to line manager approval and business needs. In customer-facing and clinical environments, flexibility is managed to maintain service continuity and patient safety, and any request must not compromise the delivery of essential services.

Attendance expectations are set out in role-specific policies and are aligned to regulatory and operational requirements. Managers are expected to monitor attendance trends, support employees returning from periods of illness, and engage early where persistent absence might affect service continuity or patient care. The group maintains a formal attendance review process and may require additional medical evidence where absence patterns suggest recurring or significant health issues.

### 5.6 Performance Management and Development

The group uses a formal performance and development framework to assess employee capability, support growth, and align individual objectives with business priorities. Annual objectives are set in discussion with line managers, and progress is reviewed at least twice per year. Performance frameworks are intended to be supportive, transparent, and linked to the business values of patient care, teamwork, integrity, and operational excellence.

Employees are encouraged to participate in learning and development programmes relevant to their role, with internal training and external qualifications recognised where appropriate. In clinical and technical roles, ongoing competence is monitored through mandatory training, supervision arrangements, and periodic revalidation where required by regulatory frameworks. The group also supports internal mobility for employees who wish to develop their careers across divisions or corporate functions.

### 5.7 Family and Carer Support

Meridian Health Group recognises that employees may have caring responsibilities and provides a range of support measures. Maternity, paternity, adoption, and shared parental leave are granted in line with statutory minimums and additional group-paid enhancements where policy allows. Employees returning from long-term leave may be supported through phased return arrangements and refresher training if required by their role.

The group also provides access to employee assistance programmes and family-focused guidance to support employees managing health, caring, or financial pressures. Managers are trained to recognise when workload, wellbeing, or personal circumstances may require adjustments and to direct employees towards support mechanisms available through HR and occupational health services.

### 5.8 Equality, Diversity and Inclusion

Meridian Health Group is committed to an inclusive workplace in which employees are treated with dignity and respect. The group prohibits discrimination, harassment, and victimisation in all forms and requires all staff to uphold the values set out in the equality and inclusion policy. The policy covers recruitment, promotion, work allocation, pay and benefits, and access to development opportunities.

The group monitors workforce diversity and inclusion indicators at divisional and corporate levels, with an annual report to the board. While strict demographic data is handled carefully to ensure lawful processing and confidentiality, the company uses this information to identify barriers to inclusion and to support fair and equitable decision-making. Management teams are expected to review inclusion outcomes regularly to ensure progress is made in recruitment, retention, and leadership representation.

## 6. Information Security Policy

This section sets out the minimum information security controls applicable to all
Meridian Health Group systems handling patient, employee, or commercially sensitive
data, and is mandatory for all employees, contractors, and third-party suppliers with
system access.

### 6.1 Access Control Principles

Access to Meridian Health Group systems is granted on a least-privilege basis and must
be approved by the relevant system owner. Standard user accounts are subject to
password rotation every 90 days and must use multi-factor authentication for any
remote access. Standard access recertification, in which line managers confirm that
their team's system access remains appropriate, is carried out twice per year, in April
and October, coordinated by the IT security function.

### 6.2 Incident Response

Any suspected information security incident must be reported to the IT security
function within one hour of discovery, via the incident hotline or the internal
reporting portal. The IT security function will triage all reported incidents within
four working hours and classify them according to the group's severity matrix, ranging
from Severity 1 (major incident, executive notification required) to Severity 4 (minor,
routine handling). All Severity 1 and Severity 2 incidents are reported to the board's
audit and risk committee at its next scheduled meeting, and, where required by
regulation, to the Information Commissioner's Office within 72 hours of discovery.

### 6.3 Privileged Access Review

Privileged accounts — defined as any account with administrative rights over
production systems, databases, or the group's identity provider — are subject to a
stricter review cadence than standard user accounts. All privileged access accounts
must be reviewed quarterly, with the fourth review of each calendar year completed no
later than 15 December, to ensure any access no longer required following role changes,
project completions, or departures has been promptly revoked ahead of the year-end
audit. Each quarterly review must be signed off by both the relevant system owner and
the Chief Information Officer, and evidence of sign-off must be retained for a minimum
of seven years to satisfy regulatory record-keeping requirements.

Any privileged account inactive for more than 30 consecutive days is automatically
suspended pending manager confirmation that continued access is required. Break-glass
emergency access procedures, used only in the event of an active incident requiring
immediate elevated access, are logged separately and reviewed by the IT security
function within 24 hours of use.

### 6.4 Data Classification and Handling Standards

Meridian Health Group classifies data according to sensitivity and regulatory exposure. Patient data, employee records, and commercially sensitive commercial information are handled under more stringent controls than general business data. Data classification levels drive storage requirements, retention periods, sharing restrictions, removable media controls, and required access approvals. Employees must not copy, transfer, or retain regulated information outside approved systems or approved secure channels.

The policy also defines requirements for record retention and deletion. Paper records containing sensitive information must be stored in locked cabinets or approved facilities; digital records must be archived in approved repositories with appropriate access logging. Disposal of records must comply with retention schedules and information governance standards, and any destruction process must be documented where required by applicable law or regulatory guidance.

### 6.5 Third-Party Access and Supplier Monitoring

Access granted to third parties, including consultants, contractors, and system integrators, must be limited to the minimum necessary to perform the agreed service. Such access is subject to contract terms, security review, and approval by system owners and the IT security function. All third-party accounts are monitored for activity, and multi-factor authentication is required for remote access to systems containing patient or commercially sensitive information.

The group performs periodic reviews of third-party arrangements to confirm that controls remain effective and that contractual commitments are being met. These reviews cover service resilience, data handling obligations, patching and vulnerability management, and any subcontracting arrangements. Where a supplier's control environment is not aligned with Meridian Health Group's expectations, the contract may be amended, access restricted, or terminated.

### 6.6 Business Continuity and Resilience

The group's information security framework is closely linked to business continuity planning. Critical systems are assessed for operational impact, recovery objectives, and dependency risks. Disaster recovery testing is conducted on a scheduled cycle, with tabletop exercises used to test escalation procedures, incident handling, and end-to-end response to cyber-related disruption.

Business continuity planning covers both operational technology and information systems used by the group. This includes clinical systems, scheduling tools, payroll and HR systems, supplier portals, and email and collaboration platforms. In the event of a prolonged outage, management must be able to restore essential services in accordance with recovery objectives while maintaining patient safety, regulatory reporting, and key commercial obligations.

## 7. Vendor and Procurement Policy

### 7.1 Supplier Onboarding

All new suppliers with access to Meridian Health Group systems or data must complete a
security and compliance questionnaire prior to onboarding, and suppliers handling
patient data must additionally provide evidence of appropriate data processing
agreements and, where applicable, current ISO 27001 or equivalent certification.
Onboarding is coordinated jointly by procurement and the IT security function, and no
supplier may be granted system access before both functions have signed off.

### 7.2 Contract Thresholds

Contracts with an annual value below £25,000 may be approved by a divisional managing
director. Contracts between £25,000 and £150,000 require additional sign-off from the
Chief Financial Officer. Any contract with an annual value above £150,000, or any
multi-year contract with a total value above £300,000, must be reviewed by the
procurement committee and approved by at least two members of the executive team.

### 7.3 Supplier Selection and Due Diligence

Supplier selection is intended to ensure the group obtains value for money while maintaining quality, compliance, and risk standards. Procurement teams consider total cost of ownership, service reliability, delivery capability, regulatory fit, and security posture when selecting vendors. For strategic suppliers or those with access to patient or business data, due diligence includes review of financial stability, quality certifications, service track records, and relevant compliance frameworks.

Where appropriate, Meridian Health Group may ask suppliers to participate in competitive tendering, negotiated procurement, or framework arrangements. For clinical equipment and software critical to patient care, the business often combines procurement discipline with operational consultation to ensure that equipment choices align with service needs, service support arrangements, and local clinical workflows. Procurement is therefore not limited to price comparison; it is a governance function supporting continuity, compliance, and patient safety.

### 7.4 Contract Management and Performance Review

Once a contract is approved, suppliers are managed through a formal performance review process. Key performance indicators may include delivery reliability, service quality, responsiveness, contract compliance, and any required security obligations. The business may use remediation plans where performance is below expectations, and in more serious cases may suspend orders or escalate the matter to executive management or the procurement committee.

Contract management also includes review of renewal options, price review clauses, service escalation processes, and the treatment of change requests. The group expects suppliers to maintain documentation and evidential records relevant to performance and compliance, and it retains the right to audit supplier operations and record-keeping where contractual or regulatory obligations require it.

### 7.5 Ethical Standards and Modern Slavery

Meridian Health Group expects suppliers to operate in line with applicable legal and ethical standards, including workplace rights, anti-bribery, anti-corruption, and modern slavery requirements. The procurement function is responsible for ensuring that suppliers are made aware of the group's expectations and that relevant clauses are included in contracts where appropriate.

The group does not tolerate bribery, facilitation payments, or unethical behaviour in supplier relationships. Any concerns regarding fraudulent conduct, coercive labour practices, or unfair contracting behaviour should be reported via the internal reporting mechanisms and will be investigated in accordance with the group's governance principles. This requirement applies to direct suppliers and any third parties working under the supplier's control.

## 9. Financial Performance — Detailed Breakdown

### 9.1 Revenue Analysis by Segment and Geography

FY2025 revenue performance reflected strong underlying demand across diagnostics and a steady occupational health base, partially offset by specialist pharmacy normalisation following prior-year expansion.

**Diagnostics Segment: £239.3m (58% of group revenue)**

The diagnostics division comprises three principal service lines: medical imaging (MRI, CT, ultrasound, X-ray), pathology and laboratory services, and occupational screening as part of diagnostic packages. Imaging services represented approximately 62% of divisional revenue, with pathology and screening making up the remainder. Volume growth of 7.8% was driven primarily by increased throughput at existing sites, expanded capacity at the Leeds and Bristol hubs, and new contracts with three NHS integrated care boards in the East Midlands and Yorkshire regions.

Geographic breakdown of diagnostics revenue:
- Manchester and North West: £98.4m (41% of segment revenue)
- Midlands (Birmingham, Coventry, Leicester): £62.1m (26% of segment revenue)
- South West and Wales (Bristol, Cardiff): £45.7m (19% of segment revenue)
- Scotland and North (Leeds, Glasgow): £32.1m (13% of segment revenue)
- International partnerships (Dublin, Rotterdam): £1.0m (0.4% of segment revenue, start-up phase)

Average imaging throughput improved 8.2% year-on-year, supported by better scheduling discipline, extended operating hours at high-utilisation sites, and improved maintenance of equipment. Pathology volumes grew more slowly at 4.1%, reflecting structural changes in NHS commissioning patterns toward consolidation of laboratory services. However, margins in pathology improved slightly due to better reagent pricing and waste reduction initiatives.

**Occupational Health Segment: £127.9m (31% of group revenue)**

The occupational health division delivered services to 267 corporate clients and 12 NHS-affiliated occupational health partnerships. Client concentration remained well-distributed, with the largest single client representing 3.1% of divisional revenue. Core service lines included pre-employment screening, periodic health surveillance, health and safety at work advisory, and employee assistance support.

Geographic and service line revenue split:
- Pre-employment and screening services: £51.2m (40% of segment revenue)
- Periodic health surveillance and statutory checks: £38.6m (30% of segment revenue)
- Health and safety advisory and counselling: £24.1m (19% of segment revenue)
- Other occupational health and management services: £14.0m (11% of segment revenue)

Growth of 4.1% was driven by contract renewals with existing large clients and incremental expansion in the Midlands and North West, where the group had won several regional NHS occupational health commissions. However, new business activity slowed in the second half of FY2025, with only two significant new contracts awarded, compared to five in the prior year. Management attributed this to tight public sector procurement cycles and extended tendering timelines for NHS framework agreements.

**Specialty Pharmacy Distribution Segment: £45.4m (11% of group revenue)**

The specialty pharmacy division distributes medicines, biologics, and related consumables to NHS trusts, private hospitals, GP practices, and other healthcare facilities. Growth of only 0.9% reflected one-off costs associated with the Warrington warehouse relocation (£2.1m in FY2025), which depressed underlying profitability. Excluding relocation-related costs, underlying revenue would have grown approximately 3.2%.

The division manages inventory of approximately 8,000 individual SKUs (stock-keeping units), covering high-cost speciality drugs, chronic disease management medicines, and associated clinical support. Inventory turns improved slightly to 4.3 times per annum from 4.1 times, reflecting better demand forecasting and improved stock management protocols.

## 10. Human Resources — Detailed Policies and Procedures

### 10.1 Recruitment and Selection

Meridian Health Group's recruitment policy is designed to attract, assess, and onboard talent who share the group's values and can contribute to organisational objectives. Recruitment decisions are made on merit and are free from discrimination.

**10.1.1 Recruitment Process**

All recruitment must follow a formal process approved by the relevant divisional managing director and coordinated by the HR function. The process comprises:

1. **Vacancy Justification** — Any new headcount or replacement recruitment must be linked to an approved business case or budget plan. Divisional managing directors approve all recruitment budgets annually in line with financial planning cycles.

2. **Role Definition** — All roles must have a current, documented job description setting out principal responsibilities, reporting lines, required competencies, and success criteria. Job descriptions must be reviewed at least annually and updated to reflect material changes in the role.

3. **Advert and Specification** — Job advertisements must be written to attract diverse candidates and clearly set out the essential and desirable attributes. Adverts must comply with equality and diversity principles and avoid language that might discourage applications from protected groups.

4. **Candidate Screening** — Initial screening is conducted by the HR function based on objective criteria defined in the role specification. Candidates progressing to interview stages will typically be assessed by a panel comprising a line manager, senior peer or clinical lead, and an HR representative.

5. **Interview and Assessment** — Interviews should be structured, with consistent questioning across all candidates. For clinical roles, practical or clinical assessments may be required. For senior roles, psychometric assessment or competency-based assessment may be used to evaluate decision-making, cultural fit, and leadership capability.

6. **Reference Checking** — Before an offer is made, a minimum of two references must be obtained. For clinical roles, at least one reference should be from a clinical supervisor. Any discrepancies or concerns raised in references must be addressed before the offer is confirmed.

7. **Pre-Employment Checks** — All successful candidates must undergo occupational health screening, Disclosure and Barring Service (DBS) checks where applicable, and verification of qualifications. For clinical roles requiring registration with professional bodies, registration must be confirmed as current and in good standing.

**10.1.2 Offer and Onboarding**

Offers of employment are made in writing by the HR function and must include the key terms of the appointment: salary, benefits, start date, probation period, and reporting line. For senior roles, the offer should be preceded by a discussion with the prospective line manager confirming mutual agreement on key expectations.

All new employees undergo an induction programme designed to familiarise them with company policies, systems, security protocols, and culture. Mandatory induction training includes information security awareness, data protection, equality and diversity, and any role-specific compliance requirements (e.g., clinical governance for clinical roles). Induction is coordinated by HR and completed within the first four weeks of employment.

Probation periods are set at three months for most roles, or six months for senior or clinical roles. At the end of probation, performance is formally reviewed, and continuation of employment is confirmed by the line manager and HR function. During probation, either party may terminate employment with one week's notice; after probation, notice periods are defined in individual contracts.

### 10.2 Remuneration and Pay Administration

**10.2.1 Pay Bands and Job Families**

Meridian Health Group uses a structured pay band system aligned to role complexity, responsibility, and market rates. The system comprises:

- **Clinical Pay Bands (C1-C6)** — Supporting radiographers, nurses, occupational health specialists, and other clinical professionals, ranging from newly qualified clinicians (C1, £24,500-£28,000) to senior specialists (C6, £42,500-£49,000).

- **Pharmacy Pay Bands (P1-P4)** — For pharmacy staff and distribution specialists, ranging from entry-level (P1, £22,500-£26,000) to senior pharmacy managers (P4, £38,000-£45,000).

- **Corporate Pay Bands (E1-E8)** — For finance, IT, HR, procurement, and administrative staff, ranging from administrative support (E1, £19,500-£23,000) to director-level roles (E8, £65,000-£90,000).

- **Senior Executive Positions** — Chief executives, Chief Financial Officer, Chief Medical Officer, Chief Information Officer, and Chief People Officer are positioned outside the pay band system, with compensation set by the board remuneration committee based on market benchmarking and company performance.

Salary bands are reviewed annually by the HR function and updated where market movements or internal equity issues are identified. Movement within a pay band is driven by performance, competency development, and role progression. Progression to a new band requires demonstration of competency at the next level and typically requires a change in role or significant expansion of responsibility.

**10.2.2 Performance Pay and Incentives**

All employees are eligible for an annual performance bonus, calculated as a percentage of base salary, subject to the achievement of both company-level and individual performance objectives. Eligible employees are typically advised of bonus potential at the start of the financial year.

- **Clinical and Corporate Staff** — Bonus potential of 10-15% of salary based on achievement of operational and financial targets specific to their division or function.

- **Senior Managers and Directors** — Bonus potential of 20-30% of salary, linked to company-level EBITDA targets, revenue growth targets, and divisional or functional metrics.

- **Executive Team** — Annual incentive plans tied to company EBITDA, revenue, margin improvement, and strategic objectives, with potential payouts ranging from 40-60% of base salary.

Bonus payments are assessed by line managers and approved by divisional heads and the finance function. All bonus decisions are recorded and form part of the compensation governance framework reviewed by the remuneration committee and external auditors annually.

**10.2.3 Overtime, Allowances and Benefits**

Where applicable, employees working in excess of their contracted hours may be eligible for overtime payment or time-off-in-lieu (TOIL) arrangements, subject to line manager approval and operational requirements. Overtime rates are typically set at 1.5x base hourly rate for standard hours and 2.0x for unsocial hours (weekend or night shifts).

Additional allowances beyond base salary include:

- **Shift Allowances** — For clinical staff working irregular shift patterns or unsocial hours (typically 10-20% uplift on base salary for night shift clinicians).

- **On-Call Allowances** — For occupational health and pharmacy staff required to maintain on-call capability, £2.50 per hour on-call (not called in) and full appropriate rate of pay if called in to work.

- **Professional Membership Fees** — The group reimburses professional registration and membership fees for clinical staff maintaining required registrations (e.g., Radiographer Registration Board, occupational health association memberships), typically up to £400-800 per annum depending on the profession.

- **Travel and Mileage** — Employees using personal vehicles for work-related travel are reimbursed at HMRC rates, currently 45p per mile up to 10,000 miles per annum, and 25p per mile thereafter.

All benefits are subject to PAYE and National Insurance treatment in accordance with HMRC guidance. The HR and Finance functions jointly administer the benefits scheme and maintain detailed records for audit and compliance purposes.

### 10.3 Leave and Absence Management

**10.3.1 Annual Leave**

Full-time employees are entitled to 25 days of annual leave per year, rising to 28 days after five continuous years of service. Annual leave accrues on a monthly basis (approximately 2.08 days per month). Part-time employees accrue leave on a pro-rata basis relative to their contracted hours.

Annual leave should be taken in accordance with operational requirements and must be approved by the line manager. The group encourages employees to take leave to maintain wellbeing, but recognises that service continuity may require some scheduling discipline. In exceptional circumstances, line managers may request that certain periods be identified as restricted leave (e.g., peak diagnostic demand periods), but employees retain the right to take their full entitlement within each financial year.

Unused annual leave cannot normally be carried forward to the following year; however, in exceptional circumstances (e.g., following a period of enforced absence or during peak operational periods), the divisional managing director may approve carry-forward of up to 5 days to the subsequent year, provided this is taken within the first quarter.

On termination of employment, employees are entitled to payment of any accrued but untaken annual leave, calculated at the employee's base salary rate at the date of departure.

**10.3.2 Sick Leave and Occupational Health**

Paid sick leave is provided to support employees who are unfit for work due to illness or injury. The framework is as follows:

- **Notification** — Employees must notify their line manager of absence as soon as practicable, normally on the first day of absence, and must provide the expected duration of absence.

- **Self-Certification** — Absences of up to seven consecutive days may be covered by employee self-certification. The company requires employees to submit a self-certification statement which is filed by HR.

- **Medical Certificate** — For absences exceeding seven consecutive days, a medical certificate from an occupational health practitioner or GP must be submitted. The company may require occupational health assessment for ongoing or recurrent absence.

- **Payment Terms** — Sick leave is paid at full salary for the first 20 working days of absence in any rolling 12-month period, and at 50% salary for a further 20 working days. Beyond this allowance, absence is unpaid unless otherwise agreed or covered by statutory disability benefits.

**10.3.3 Maternity, Paternity and Parental Leave**

Meridian Health Group provides enhanced parental leave arrangements beyond statutory minimums:

- **Maternity Leave** — Women are entitled to 16 weeks of paid maternity leave (at 100% of salary) followed by an additional 20 weeks at statutory maternity pay rates. The first 16 weeks are considered occupational benefit and do not reduce statutory entitlements.

- **Paternity Leave** — Male and non-pregnant partners are entitled to two weeks of paid paternity leave (at 100% salary) within the first six months following the birth of a child.

- **Shared Parental Leave** — Eligible parents may elect to share a total of 50 weeks of leave (in addition to maternity leave) within the first year following birth. Leave can be taken flexibly and is paid at statutory rates.

- **Adoption Leave** — Employees adopting a child are entitled to equivalent benefits to maternity leave, comprising 16 weeks paid and 20 weeks at statutory rates.

- **Return to Work** — Employees returning from parental leave are restored to their original role or a similar role at the same level. Flexible working arrangements may be requested upon return and will be considered in accordance with Section 10.3.4.

Detailed information regarding leave entitlements, notification procedures, and interaction with occupational benefits is provided in the HR policies guide, available from the HR function.

### 10.4 Flexible Working and Remote Work

**10.4.1 Policy Framework**

Meridian Health Group recognises that flexible working arrangements can support employee wellbeing, retention, and productivity, while maintaining operational effectiveness. Flexible arrangements must not compromise patient safety, service delivery, or clinical governance standards. Requests for flexible working are considered individually and may be approved, approved with conditions, or declined based on operational necessity.

Requests should be made in writing to the line manager and include details of the proposed arrangement, expected start date, and business case for the change. The line manager will discuss the request with the employee, consult with the divisional managing director if necessary, and provide a written response within 10 working days. If the request is declined, the manager must provide clear operational reasons.

**10.4.2 Flexible Working Arrangements**

Approved flexible working arrangements may include:

- **Compressed Hours** — Working full-time hours over fewer days (e.g., 40 hours over 4 days) or in shorter weeks. This is particularly suitable for corporate and administrative roles.

- **Job Sharing** — Two employees sharing a single full-time role, with each working part-time. Both employees must meet performance standards and coverage must be maintained.

- **Hybrid/Remote Working** — Working from a combination of office and remote locations. For corporate roles, this is often feasible; for clinical roles, it is restricted to administrative, supervisory, or support functions.

- **Term-Time Working** — Working full hours during term time but reduced hours during school holidays. This is typically used for parents with school-age children and is subject to operational feasibility.

- **Phased Retirement** — Reducing hours or responsibility in advance of retirement, to facilitate knowledge transfer and support business continuity.

**10.4.3 Remote Work Standards**

Employees approved for remote or hybrid working are expected to maintain the same professional standards as office-based employees. Key requirements include:

- Maintaining a secure, distraction-free workspace compliant with data security policies
- Ensuring appropriate social interaction and team engagement
- Responding to communications within agreed response times
- Attending essential in-person meetings and team events
- Maintaining accurate time and attendance records

The company may revoke remote working approval if operational needs change or if performance standards are not maintained. Remote workers must also comply with information security policies, including the use of virtual private networks (VPNs), multi-factor authentication, and restricted access to sensitive systems.

### 10.5 Disciplinary and Grievance Procedures

**10.5.1 Disciplinary Policy**

Meridian Health Group aims to maintain professional standards and resolve conduct issues fairly and consistently. Disciplinary action is reserved for material breaches of policy, gross misconduct, or persistent underperformance. Minor issues are typically addressed through supportive discussion and feedback.

**Levels of Disciplinary Action:**

1. **Informal Warning** — For minor, first-time breaches, the line manager may issue an informal warning and document the discussion. No formal record is retained after 3 months if there are no further incidents.

2. **Formal Written Warning** — For more serious or repeated breaches, a formal written warning is issued, specifying the breach, expected standards, and consequences of further breach. This remains on record for 12 months.

3. **Final Written Warning** — For serious or repeated breaches following a written warning, a final written warning is issued. This notice remains on file for 12 months and makes clear that further serious breach will result in dismissal.

4. **Dismissal** — Termination may follow a final written warning if the employee commits a further serious breach. Dismissal may also be applied immediately for gross misconduct (theft, violence, serious safety violation, breach of confidentiality affecting patient safety).

**Disciplinary Procedure:**

Formal disciplinary action requires:
- Written notice of the allegation and proposed hearing, with at least 10 working days' notice
- A hearing before a manager senior to the line manager (typically divisional managing director)
- The employee's right to be accompanied by a work colleague or union representative
- An opportunity for the employee to present their account and response
- A documented decision and right of appeal

Appeals must be submitted within 10 working days and are heard by a senior manager not involved in the original decision.

**10.5.2 Grievance Procedure**

Employees with workplace concerns are encouraged to raise these initially with their line manager or HR function informally, to seek early resolution. If informal resolution is not possible, a formal grievance may be lodged.

**Formal Grievance Process:**

1. **Submission** — The employee submits a written grievance to HR, setting out the nature of the complaint, individuals involved, dates, and desired outcome. HR acknowledges receipt within 2 working days.

2. **Hearing** — A grievance hearing is arranged within 10 working days with a manager senior to the employee's line manager. The employee may be accompanied by a colleague or representative.

3. **Investigation** — The company investigates the complaint, including interviews with relevant parties and review of documentation.

4. **Decision** — A written decision is issued within 10 working days of the hearing, setting out findings and any remedial action.

5. **Appeal** — If the employee is dissatisfied, they may appeal within 10 working days to a more senior manager or the Chief People Officer.

## 11. Information Security — Comprehensive Framework

### 11.1 Information Classification and Handling

**11.1.1 Classification Levels**

Meridian Health Group implements a four-tier data classification framework:

**Level 1 — Public Information**
- General marketing materials, published annual reports, policy summaries
- No confidentiality restrictions; may be shared externally
- Example: Public website content, published press releases

**Level 2 — Internal Use Only**
- Internal memos, general HR announcements, non-sensitive operational data
- Restricted to employees and contractors; should not be disclosed to external parties without consent
- Example: Internal announcements, general business updates, non-financial operational metrics

**Level 3 — Confidential**
- Commercially sensitive information, financial data, strategy documents, patient data that is de-identified but not anonymous, employee personal data (names, addresses, contact information)
- Restricted to employees with business need; requires access controls and audit logging
- Example: Financial forecasts, contract terms, internal audit reports, general HR records, payroll information

**Level 4 — Highly Confidential / Restricted**
- Personally identifiable patient health information, special category employee data (health, disciplinary), board-level strategic information, cryptographic keys and credentials
- Strictly limited access on least-privilege basis; requires strong access controls, encryption, and comprehensive audit trails
- Example: Patient records, medical histories, employee health records, financial accounts and banking details, system passwords

### 11.2 Access Control and Identity Management

**11.2.1 Principle of Least Privilege**

Access to company systems and data is granted on a least-privilege basis, meaning employees receive only the access required to perform their duties. No employee should have access to systems, data, or functionality beyond their job requirements.

Access approval is the responsibility of the line manager and system owner. Access is granted using standardized role-based access controls (RBAC), where permissions are assigned based on job function rather than individual need. Role definitions are maintained by the IT security function and reviewed at least annually or when significant job role changes occur.

**11.2.2 User Account Lifecycle**

| Stage | Policy | Owner |
|-------|--------|-------|
| **Provisioning** | New user accounts are created on day 1 of employment. Accounts are provisioned with multi-factor authentication enabled. Initial credentials are single-use and must be changed at first login. | IT Service Desk / HR |
| **Standard Access** | Standard user access is assigned based on role definition and is logged and approved by line manager. Multi-factor authentication is required for any remote access. | System Owner / IT |
| **Privileged Access** | Privileged accounts (with administrative rights) are provisioned only upon specific written request from the divisional managing director, with documented business justification. Privileged users are trained on security obligations and logging. | IT Security / CIO |
| **Access Review** | Standard user access is reviewed twice per year (April and October) by line managers, confirming that access remains appropriate. Privileged accounts are reviewed quarterly, with the year-end review (by 15 Dec) signed off by system owner and CIO. | Line Manager / System Owner |
| **Deprovisioning** | On departure, user accounts are disabled on the departure date. Data is preserved per retention policy. Account deletions occur after 90 days if no ongoing obligations exist. | HR / IT Service Desk |

**11.2.3 Password and Credential Management**

All user accounts are protected by a strong password meeting the following criteria:

- Minimum 12 characters
- Combination of uppercase, lowercase, numeric, and special characters
- Not based on dictionary words or personal information
- Not reused across multiple accounts or systems

Passwords must be changed every 90 days for standard users and every 60 days for privileged users. Password history is maintained to prevent reuse of recent passwords. Users should not share passwords with colleagues or write passwords in insecure locations. System administrators should not request passwords from users; password changes should be managed through self-service or authenticated help desk channels.

For system-to-system connections and service accounts, credentials should be managed through secure credential stores (e.g., vaults) and rotated at least quarterly or when personnel changes occur.

**11.2.4 Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)**

Multi-factor authentication is mandatory for:
- All remote access (VPN, cloud services)
- Privileged accounts and administrative access
- Access to systems containing patient or highly sensitive data

MFA implementations typically use one of the following factors:
- Something you know (password)
- Something you have (security token, authenticator app, hardware key)
- Something you are (biometric)

Acceptable MFA methods include authenticator applications (Microsoft Authenticator, Google Authenticator), hardware security keys, or SMS-based codes (though SMS is considered lower assurance). Mobile push approvals through authenticator apps are preferred.

### 11.3 Network Security and System Access

**11.3.1 Network Architecture**

Meridian Health Group implements a network security architecture with segmentation to separate clinical systems, corporate systems, and external-facing services. Key network security controls include:

- **Firewalls** — Perimeter firewalls restrict inbound and outbound traffic to approved protocols and ports. Internal firewalls segment clinical and corporate networks.

- **Virtual Private Network (VPN)** — Remote workers and offsite staff must connect via VPN to access internal systems. VPN access is encrypted and requires multi-factor authentication.

- **Intrusion Detection and Prevention** — Network monitoring systems detect unusual traffic patterns and potential intrusions. Alerts are reviewed by the IT security team.

- **Data Loss Prevention (DLP)** — DLP solutions monitor network traffic for attempts to exfiltrate sensitive data via email, file transfers, or cloud services.

**11.3.2 Endpoint Security**

All company-owned devices (laptops, desktops, tablets, phones) are enrolled in Mobile Device Management (MDM) and must comply with minimum security standards:

- Antivirus/anti-malware protection (automatically updated)
- Firewall enabled
- Automatic screen lock after 10 minutes of inactivity
- Full disk encryption
- Current security patches and OS updates
- Prohibition of unauthorised software installation

Users are responsible for maintaining device security, reporting lost or stolen devices immediately, and not connecting company devices to untrusted networks or USB devices.

| **Initial Triage** | IT security reviews ticket and determines severity classification. Contacts employee for additional context. Assesses if incident affects critical systems or patient safety. If Severity 1-2, escalates to Chief Information Officer and relevant divisional lead. | IT Security Team |
| **Containment** | If malware suspected: isolate affected device, scan and remediate. If unauthorised access suspected: disable account, reset credentials, review access logs. If data breach suspected: preserve evidence, avoid overwriting logs, engage forensic investigation if warranted. | IT Security / System Owner |
| **Investigation** | Engage forensics team if Severity 1-2. Review system and access logs to determine scope: who was affected, what data was accessed, when did access occur, how was access obtained. Document findings in incident log. | IT Security / Forensics Team |
| **Communication** | For Severity 1-2 incidents affecting patient data: notify Information Commissioner's Office within 72 hours if breach is reportable. Notify affected individuals if personal data has been compromised. Board audit committee is notified at next scheduled meeting. | Chief Information Officer / Legal |
| **Remediation** | Implement fixes to prevent recurrence (patch systems, disable weak accounts, update access controls). Document root cause and corrective actions. Implement monitoring to detect similar incidents. | IT Security / System Owner |
| **Post-Incident Review** | Conduct lessons-learned meeting with stakeholders. Document findings and actions in incident log. Brief executive team on lessons. Share anonymised findings with staff as learning opportunity. | Chief Information Officer |

**11.4.3 Forensic Investigation Guidelines**

In the event of suspected data breach or intrusion, a forensic investigation may be required. Key principles include:

- **Preserve Evidence** — Do not power down affected systems unless necessary to prevent further harm. Preserve system memory and disk images for forensic analysis.

- **Chain of Custody** — Document who accessed evidence, when, and for what purpose. Maintain physical and digital security of evidence.

- **Engagement of External Experts** — For serious incidents, engage external forensic specialists (typically from the company's cyber insurance provider).

- **Regulatory Liaison** — Cooperate with law enforcement if criminal activity is suspected (e.g., ransomware, data theft).

### 12.3 Procurement Case Studies

**Case Study 1: Diagnostic Equipment Procurement (Category A)**

In FY2025, Meridian Health Group procured three MRI systems and five CT scanners to support expanded diagnostics capacity across Manchester, Leeds, and Bristol hubs. This multi-year procurement involved significant capital expenditure (£16.1m approved budget) and required rigorous supplier management.

**Process:**
1. Clinical teams defined specifications: MRI systems with 3T field strength, CT scanners with latest reconstruction algorithms, expected throughput (80 patients/day per MRI, 120/day per CT).
2. Three vendors were invited to participate in competitive tender: Siemens, Philips, and GE Healthcare.
3. Evaluation criteria included: price (40%), technical capability (30%), service support and training (20%), and sustainability/energy efficiency (10%).
4. Site visits were conducted, and vendor demonstrations were observed by clinical and engineering teams.
5. Siemens was selected based on optimal price-quality profile and committed 18-month implementation with local service center.

**Key Contract Terms:**
- Total contract value: £14.2m (equipment and installation)
- Payment: 30% on order, 50% on delivery, 20% on successful commissioning
- Service agreement: 5-year warranty with on-site parts availability and 4-hour response time for critical failures
- Training: 200 hours of operator and technician training included
- Trade-in: Meridian returned legacy equipment valued at £2.1m credit against purchase price

**Outcomes:**
- All systems commissioned on schedule (18 months)
- Throughput targets exceeded: MRI achieving 85 patients/day, CT achieving 125 patients/day
- Service response time targets met in 98% of cases
- Training program well-received; staff competency assessments passed by 96% of trainees

**Lessons Learned:**
- Rigorous clinical input during specification phase prevented requirement for retrofits or changes post-installation
- Staged payment tied to milestones enforced supplier accountability
- Strong relationship management with vendor senior leadership escalated supply chain delays during global chip shortage period and ensured priority allocation

**Case Study 2: Cloud Infrastructure Migration (Category A)**

Meridian Health Group undertook a multi-year migration of on-premises infrastructure to Microsoft Azure, supporting the clinical data platform and corporate systems. This project involved significant technical complexity, data security considerations, and business continuity risk.

**Scope:**
- Migrate 45 TB of data across patient records, imaging archives, occupational health records
- Move 200+ virtual machines and applications to cloud infrastructure
- Establish hybrid cloud model with on-premises failover for critical systems
- Achieve compliance with NHS data residency requirements and HIPAA equivalence

**Supplier Selection:**
- Microsoft was selected as cloud provider based on: healthcare compliance certifications (NHS approved, HIPAA aligned), European data center availability (Ireland), proven security controls, and competitive pricing.
- Accenture was selected as systems integrator based on: experience with healthcare migrations, presence in UK/Ireland, strong security posture, and fixed-price engagement model.

**Contract Framework:**
- Microsoft: 3-year cloud services agreement with commitment for 500GB incremental storage; auto-renewal with 90-day cancellation notice
- Accenture: Fixed-price engagement (£2.1m) with milestone-based payment; includes data migration, testing, and training
- Both suppliers executed Data Processing Agreements (DPAs) confirming compliance with data residency and processing restrictions

**Implementation Timeline:**
- Phase 1 (Months 1-4): Infrastructure setup, security controls validation, pilot migration of non-critical systems
- Phase 2 (Months 5-10): Migration of core clinical systems with parallel operation and fallback testing
- Phase 3 (Months 11-12): Migration of remaining systems, cutover, and production validation

**Security Measures Implemented:**
- All data encrypted in transit (TLS 1.2) and at rest (AES-256)
- Hybrid cloud architecture with on-premises failover data center
- Network segmentation between clinical, corporate, and non-sensitive systems
- Access logging to Azure; 90-day retention with archival to 7-year storage
- Quarterly security assessments and penetration testing by third-party firm

**Outcomes:**
- Migration completed within 12-month timeline and £2.1m fixed-price budget
- Zero security incidents during migration
- Disaster recovery drills completed successfully; failover time reduced from 4 hours to 1.5 hours
- System availability improved to 99.95% (SLA target 99.9%)
- Estimated £1.2m annual cost savings from reduced on-premises infrastructure costs and staff

**Performance Management:**
- Monthly steering committee meetings with Microsoft and Accenture reviewing milestones and risk register
- Service level dashboard tracks uptime, response times, and patch deployment; reported to CIO and divisional leads
- Annual business review confirms continued strategic fit and identifies optimization opportunities
